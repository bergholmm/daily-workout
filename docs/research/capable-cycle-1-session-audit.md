# CAPABLE Cycle 1 — session audit

## Scope and verdict

This audit reviews the exact proposed prescriptions in
[`docs/capable-cycle-1-spec.md`](../capable-cycle-1-spec.md). It asks whether
the nine sessions are coherent applications of current evidence. It does **not**
claim that this exact 12-week program has been validated in a trial; it has not.
The detailed findings below document the initial draft; the post-revision
section records the result after those findings were incorporated.

Initial-draft verdict: **the structure is defensible, but the specification is not
ready to implement unchanged**. The Lower sessions and Conditioning 1 are
mostly sound. Upper 1–3 need clearer pulling prescriptions, and Upper 2–3 need
less volume. All primary strength blocks need explicit rest. The cardiovascular
and bone-health language also needs to match what one weekly conditioning
session can actually deliver.

## Post-revision re-audit — 11 August 2026

**Verdict: ready to implement as an evidence-informed pilot.** The revision
resolves the initial draft's concrete programming blockers: the pull-up rungs
are executable and separately recordable; squat and pull-up rest is explicit;
Upper 2 and Upper 3 fit realistic set and time caps; loaded pancake work has an
RPE ceiling and permanent regression; Lower adds a second low-dose impact
contact; Conditioning progression is earned; the technically novel Phase 3
clean has familiar fallbacks; recovery spacing and transition time are
explicit; and the cardiovascular and bone-health claims are narrower.

| Revised session | Verdict | Reason                                                                                                                          |
| --------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lower 1         | Pass    | Conservative calibration, two accessory rounds, explicit rest, impact fallback, and elevated pancake scaling are coherent.      |
| Upper 1         | Pass    | Duplicate pulling was removed; hold volume, pull-up dosing, and accessory volume now fit the stated intent.                     |
| Conditioning 1  | Pass    | Full-body second exposure, bounded intensity, earned fourth round, and landing fallbacks remain intact.                         |
| Lower 2         | Pass    | Specific strength continuity and controlled end-range progression are retained without excessive accessory volume.              |
| Upper 2         | Pass    | The revised `3 / 2 / 2` skill work, three-set pull-up anchor, two accessory rounds, and real rest are feasible.                 |
| Conditioning 2  | Pass    | Two-hand swings are the default and the fifth round plus unilateral variation must be earned.                                   |
| Lower 3         | Pass    | The calibration exposure, two-round assistance, RPE 5–6 pancake ceiling, and elevated regression contain fatigue appropriately. |
| Upper 3         | Pass    | Complete muscle-ups are capped at 4–6 clean singles, dips are conditional, and the final accessories are reduced.               |
| Conditioning 3  | Pass    | The circuit uses the familiar goblet squat and two-hand swing. It does not add a new clean variation under fatigue.             |

No remaining safety or feasibility issue requires a programming change before
implementation. Three boundaries should remain visible in product copy:

- The exact nine-session design is **evidence-informed, not clinically or
  experimentally validated**.
- One formal handstand exposure supports deliberate practice but is a modest
  acquisition dose. Faster skill learning is more likely to require the
  optional short practice exposure; no direct adult trial validates that exact
  dose.
- Sessions 2 and 3 place the squat near roughly 81–83% of current e1RM, with
  RPE and technique retaining veto power. The wave now contains meaningful
  heavy work but is still not a high-volume heavy-strength protocol.

## Movement-first re-audit — 12 August 2026

The implemented pilot was checked again after the program identity moved away
from a squat-centered design.

**Verdict: all nine workout definitions remain suitable for pilot use.** The
back squat is now a two-to-three-set loaded support block. Pull-up, press, row,
push-up, hinge, one-leg strength, carries, locomotion, impact or landing, and
trunk control remain visible across every phase. Lower and Conditioning retain
two weekly pancake exposures. Upper retains handstand and bar muscle-up work in
all three phases. Front lever is not included in Cycle 1.

The phase transitions preserve movement families:

- Phase 1 selects positions and suitable skill levels.
- Phase 2 adds useful range, load, or connected skill work.
- Phase 3 uses stronger or more complete versions with earlier fallbacks.

The automated program audit checks nine unique phase/workout slots, four weekly
progressions, full phase-level movement-pattern coverage, two pancake
exposures, continued gymnastics focuses, and the absence of chin-up, front
lever, fixed YouTube videos, and embedded YouTube videos.

This re-audit does not change the evidence boundary. The design is
evidence-informed. The exact twelve-week cycle must still be tested through
real sessions before its exercise choices, time estimates, and progressions can
be treated as settled.

The copy cleanup is also complete: the relevant blocks now use **"Impact and
landing exposure"** rather than implying a validated bone mineral-density
outcome.

### Rating key

- **Pass:** coherent as written; retain normal individual scaling.
- **Caution:** viable, but a named ambiguity or load-management issue should be
  resolved before publication.
- **Change required:** the written prescription has a material feasibility,
  progression, or safety problem that should be corrected before implementation.

## Evidence-supported principles used in the audit

- Current ACSM guidance reports that strength is enhanced by heavier loading
  (at least 80% 1RM), full range of motion, 2–3 sets, priority exercises early
  in the session, and at least two weekly exposures. Training to momentary
  failure is not consistently necessary
  ([Currier et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/)).
- One equal-volume weekly exposure can still produce gains in a less-trained
  population, but that finding comes from a small elbow-flexor study and should
  not be mistaken for a general optimum
  ([Gentil et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24732784/)).
- Exercises trained first tend to receive the greatest strength benefit, so
  goal-priority work should be placed early
  ([Simão et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3737971/)).
- A randomized trial found better strength and hypertrophy outcomes with
  three-minute rather than one-minute rests during a multi-exercise resistance
  program in trained men
  ([Schoenfeld et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/)). This
  does not make three minutes mandatory for every exercise, but it argues
  against leaving rest undefined for the squat and heavy pull-up work.
- ACSM recommends flexibility training at least twice weekly, accumulating
  about 60 seconds per target exercise
  ([Garber et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556/)). Full-range
  resistance training can also improve range of motion
  ([Morton et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21969080/)).
- Concurrent resistance and conditioning can improve both strength and aerobic
  outcomes when dose and recovery are managed
  ([Lee et al., 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0233134)).
- For adult bone health, ACSM recommends resistance exercise 2–3 times weekly
  and weight-bearing/impact activity 3–5 times weekly
  ([Kohrt et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15514517/)).
- WHO's health target is 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or
  75–150 minutes of vigorous activity weekly, plus strengthening of all major
  muscle groups on at least two days
  ([WHO, 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK566046/)).
- World Gymnastics emphasizes gradual load, foundational shapes and strength,
  and progression before difficult elements
  ([World Gymnastics Age Group Programme](https://www.worldgymnastics.sport/site/pages/education-agegroup.php)).
  Direct trials do not define universal adult prerequisites or an optimal dose
  for handstands or bar muscle-ups.

The exact set counts, exercise substitutions, RPE cutoffs, skill ladders, and
time caps recommended below are **programming inferences** built within those
constraints.

## Initial-draft program-level audit (superseded by the revision above)

### Weekly movement and muscle frequency — pass with a condition

The split does not behave as three isolated body-part days:

- Lower provides the primary squat, hinge, and unilateral work; Conditioning
  adds a second squat/lunge/hinge and carry exposure.
- Upper provides vertical pull, press, row, and gymnastics; Conditioning adds
  a second push and pull exposure.
- Pancake work appears in Lower and Conditioning.

That satisfies the intended two-contact structure. The condition is that the
Conditioning circuits remain genuinely loaded and technically controlled. If
they become light cardio-only work, the program loses its second weekly
strength exposure.

### Per-session ordering — pass

The proposed order is appropriate: preparation, fresh skill or impact work,
priority strength, assistance/capacity, then relaxed range. Handstands and
muscle-up drills occur before fatigue, jumps occur before circuits, and no
complete muscle-up is hidden inside conditioning. Preserve this order.

### Resistance volume, intensity, and rest — change required

The back-squat wave is conservative and eventually reaches a genuinely heavy
zone: 90–92.5% of a 90% training max is approximately 81–83% e1RM. Its normal
work uses two or three sets, consistent with the ACSM evidence synthesis.
However, the spec gives the squat 18 minutes without prescribing interset rest.
Write **2.5–4 minutes between squat work sets** and **2–3 minutes around the
calibration set** explicitly. Use at least 2 minutes for hard pull-up sets and
roughly 60–90 seconds for low-fatigue skill drills and paired accessories.

Upper-body volume is the larger issue. Upper 2 prescribes ten muscle-up
preparation sets in eleven minutes before four pull-up sets and three row sets.
Upper 3 prescribes 6–10 muscle-up sets plus three dip sets in twelve minutes,
then pull-ups, pike handstand push-ups, and rows. Those blocks cannot receive
useful rest and fit the stated duration. Fatigue is also concentrated at the
wrist, elbow, and shoulder.

Before implementation:

1. Reduce the pull-up anchor to the same `3 / 3 / top+2 / 2` work-set shape as
   the squat wave.
2. Define a combined per-session cap for hard high-pull, complete muscle-up,
   pull-up, and dip work instead of letting each block progress independently.
3. Add explicit rest and remove sets until Upper sessions fit on paper **with
   4–6 minutes of equipment transitions**.

### Back-squat e1RM and training-max wave — caution

The Brzycki equation has empirical use for lower-repetition sets, and lower-rep
tests generally estimate 1RM better than higher-repetition tests
([Mayhew et al., 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7500624/);
[Reynolds et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16937972/)). The spec
correctly labels e1RM as an estimate, uses a 90% training max, limits effective
reps, avoids failure, and lets RPE veto the percentage.

The RIR adjustment remains an inference because the classical equation assumes
repetitions to fatigue. Novices also judge RIR less reliably than experienced
lifters
([Zourdos et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26049792/);
[Steele et al., 2017](https://peerj.com/articles/4105/)). Keep the rule that a
novice does not add estimated RIR. Clarify that there is **one calibration set,
not repeated attempts to find the perfect RPE**, and identify exactly which two
observations confirm a new TM. A calibration-day Lower session also needs
reduced accessory volume or a longer published duration.

### Pull-up anchor and bar muscle-up ladder — change required

The qualitative bar muscle-up gates are appropriately conservative: high pull,
stable support, pain-free turnover, and a regression always available. They are
coaching criteria, not validated universal thresholds, and should stay labeled
that way.

The pull-up ladder is not yet a single executable prescription. “4 × 5” means
very different tissue stress for a band-assisted pull-up, a long eccentric plus
isometric, a bodyweight pull-up, and a weighted pull-up. Define each rung:

- assisted/bodyweight/weighted: prescribed clean repetitions and RPE;
- eccentric: number of repetitions, eccentric duration, and isometric position;
- support or dip: separate dosage and stop rule.

Do not equate a five-second eccentric with one ordinary repetition in the data
model. Reduce assistance or add load only after the same rung is completed
cleanly across the phase; do not combine a new bar-muscle-up variation with an
increase in hard pulling volume.

### Handstand dose and placement — caution

Placement is good. An 8–10 minute block before upper strength is short enough
to preserve quality, and the box/wall fallbacks prevent completion from
depending on a freestanding skill. The exact dose is a coaching inference.
General motor-learning work supports spaced rather than massed exposure, but a
simple laboratory task cannot determine a gymnastics prescription
([Kwon et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4395711/)).

One formal exposure per week is likely to produce slower skill learning than
several short exposures. Keep the optional practices, but describe them as the
recommended route for someone who prioritizes handstand acquisition. For a new
handstand trainee, cap the first phase at roughly **60–120 seconds of total
weight-bearing hold time**, progressing only if wrists and shoulders remain
settled. Adult handstand evidence is observational rather than prescriptive,
but wrist loading and chronic pain warrant gradual exposure
([Martonovich et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12550924/)).

### Pancake and flexibility dose — pass with a condition

The plan provides at least two weekly pancake exposures and at least 60 seconds
of relaxed range, while also training active compression and controlled loaded
range. That is consistent with general flexibility guidance. The condition is
that the loaded straddle good morning remains assistance work, not a second
heavy hinge after RDLs. Give it an explicit **RPE 5–6 ceiling** and preserve the
elevated-seat fallback in every phase, including Phase 3.

### Conditioning, aerobic capacity, impact, and bone loading — change required

The E5MOM circuits are well designed for repeatability: they include lower,
push, pull, and carry work; use RPE 6–7; and create built-in rest. The impact
blocks are also sensibly placed before fatigue and include non-jumping
fallbacks.

The dose does not support the strongest product language. One 15–25 minute
mixed circuit is far below WHO's weekly aerobic target. One weekly impact block
is also below ACSM's bone-loading frequency guidance. CAPABLE can truthfully
say it contributes to work capacity and bone-loading exposure, but not that the
three sessions alone constitute complete cardiovascular or bone-health
training.

Choose one of these before implementation:

- add a second low-dose impact exposure (for example 2 × 5 low pogos or a
  non-impact fallback after Lower preparation) and make regular brisk walking
  or 1–2 easy 20–40 minute aerobic sessions the recommended companion; or
- retain the current dose and narrow the claims explicitly.

The proposed optional 4 × 4 interval substitution should not appear as a
generic option for novice users unless its mode, work intensity, recovery, and
readiness criteria are defined.

### Recovery and self-paced behavior — change required

The order Lower → Upper → Conditioning is sensible, but “with recovery where
practical” is too permissive for a self-paced product. Use a clear default:

- normally leave at least one non-training day between formal sessions;
- do not stack all three formal sessions on consecutive days to catch up; and
- after a five-round Conditioning session with jumping, leave roughly 48 hours
  before the next Lower session.

These exact spacing rules are conservative programming inferences, not trial-
validated cutoffs. They make the intended Monday/Wednesday/Friday recovery
rhythm legible without calendar-locking the program.

### Session duration — change required

The printed block times sum to 54–56 minutes for most Lower and Upper sessions
before changing equipment, loading barbells, recording results, or unexpected
rest. Assessment days require additional warm-up and calculation time. The
55–60 minute promise is therefore not credible for all exposures as written.

Either publish **55–65 minutes** for Lower/Upper, or remove enough accessory
sets to leave a real 5-minute transition buffer. Keep Conditioning at 45–55
minutes; its arithmetic is feasible. A calibration-day Lower session should
automatically use two accessory rounds.

## Initial-draft session-by-session decisions (superseded)

### Lower 1 — Squat Base & Elevated Pancake: **Caution**

What works:

- Squat is correctly placed before RDL and unilateral work.
- Squat, hinge, unilateral strength, active range, and relaxed range are all
  represented without exercise novelty for its own sake.
- The elevated pancake start matches the stated beginner.

Resolve before publication:

- State squat rest explicitly.
- When the e1RM set occurs, use two hinge/unilateral rounds and publish a
  60–65-minute assessment duration; the current 18-minute strength allocation
  is optimistic.
- Keep elevated straddle good mornings unweighted or at RPE 5–6 in Phase 1.

### Upper 1 — Handstand Line & Muscle-Up Foundations: **Change required**

What works:

- Wrist/scapular preparation and handstand placement are appropriate.
- Low-bar transition and support work are suitable regressions.
- Press and row prevent the session from becoming vertical-pull-only.

Change:

- Define the eccentric/isometric pull-up rung separately from ordinary reps.
- Move active hang/scapular pull into preparation or use only two foundation
  sets; it currently duplicates the later four-set pull anchor.
- Use three pull-up work sets, at least two minutes rest for hard sets, and a
  first-phase handstand hold cap. Re-time the session with transitions.

### Conditioning 1 — Landing & Repeatable Capacity: **Pass**

This is the strongest session in the draft. Impact occurs while fresh, the
circuit provides the second weekly full-body resistance exposure, intensity is
bounded, fallbacks are explicit, and pancake receives its second dose.

Retain one progression condition: add the fourth round only if Exposure 1
finished within the RPE target with consistent rounds and no adverse landing or
joint response. A 3-to-4-round increase is a 33% volume jump, so it should be
earned rather than automatic.

### Lower 2 — Squat Strength & Lower Pancake: **Caution**

What works:

- The same squat preserves specificity across phases.
- RDL plus rear-foot-elevated split squat develops posterior and unilateral
  strength, and pancake advances only through controlled range.

Resolve:

- Add squat rest and keep the calibration-day two-round accessory rule.
- Allow two accessory rounds when the five-round Conditioning 2 dose creates
  persistent lower-body fatigue.
- Cap the loaded straddle work at RPE 5–6; lowering support and adding load in
  the same exposure is prohibited.

### Upper 2 — Handstand Balance & Muscle-Up Integration: **Change required**

What works:

- Handstand moves from line toward balance with a line fallback.
- High pull, transition, and support/dip address recognizable components of a
  bar muscle-up.

Change:

- Ten bar-skill sets in eleven minutes is not credible. Use approximately
  `3 × 3` high pulls, `2 × 3` transitions, and `2 × 4–6` supported dips with
  60–120 seconds according to difficulty.
- Reduce the pull-up anchor to three work sets and press/row to two rounds when
  the hardest bar-skill variation is used.
- Progress either pull height, transition assistance, or total clean reps—not
  one variable in every drill during the same exposure.

### Conditioning 2 — Unilateral Strength Capacity: **Caution**

What works:

- Impact remains early and scaled.
- Lunge, hinge, press, row, carry, lateral range, and pancake create a useful
  whole-body second exposure.

Resolve:

- Make the two-hand swing the default until the trainee has repeatable
  one-arm mechanics on both sides; a single-arm ballistic hinge is not merely
  an intensity scale.
- Earn the fifth round through consistent RPE and recovery. Five rounds include
  60 loaded reverse-lunge repetitions, so the lower-body response after Lower 2
  matters more than the calendar week.

### Lower 3 — Squat Expression & Active Pancake: **Caution**

What works:

- The final strength estimate stays submaximal.
- The floor pancake is explicitly optional.
- Loaded Cossack work expresses lateral strength instead of adding another
  unrelated lift.

Resolve:

- Add squat rest and reduce accessories to two rounds on the calibration
  exposure.
- RDL at RPE 8, loaded Cossack squats, and loaded end-range good mornings in
  one session can make the final block a fatigue test. Cap pancake strength at
  RPE 5–6 and regress support immediately when pelvic or spinal control changes.

### Upper 3 — Handstand & Bar Muscle-Up Expression: **Change required**

What works:

- Freestanding and unassisted attempts are optional and regressions remain
  valid outcomes.
- Complete skills occur before general strength and never inside conditioning.

Change:

- `6–10` muscle-up sets plus three dip sets cannot fit into twelve minutes with
  high-quality rest. Use **4–6 sets**, cap complete work at **4–6 clean total
  repetitions**, and use no more than two dip sets; omit the dips when they
  materially reduce turnover quality or the trainee's elbows or shoulders are
  not settled.
- Use two or three pull-up work sets. If the athlete performs the hardest
  complete-muscle-up variation, use only two press/row rounds; the session does
  not need maximal muscle-up, pull-up, dip, handstand-push-up, and row volume at
  once.
- Allocate at least 15 minutes to the revised muscle-up block or widen the
  session duration. Repeated misses, slow turnovers, or painful support end the
  complete-movement work.

### Conditioning 3 — Integrated Full-Body Expression: **Change required**

What works:

- Jumping is early, the circuit stays at RPE 7, and pulls cannot fail.
- The carry and second pancake/compression dose support the program's broader
  goals.

Change:

- Double-kettlebell clean plus front squat is a new technical demand under
  repeated fatigue, but no explicit fallback is supplied. Require demonstrated
  clean mechanics or use the already learned two-hand swing plus goblet squat,
  or a goblet clean/front squat.
- Keep four rounds until every movement remains consistent. Add the fifth only
  when the clean/squat, pulls, and landings all retain their quality—not merely
  when session RPE is low.
- This session remains a capacity benchmark, not a substitute for the weekly
  aerobic volume recommended for health.

## Initial-draft required edits (resolved in the revised specification)

1. Add explicit main-lift and pull-up rest intervals.
2. Rewrite the pull-up ladder so assisted reps, eccentrics/isometrics,
   bodyweight reps, and weighted reps have executable, separately recorded
   prescriptions.
3. Reduce and cap Upper 2 and Upper 3 bar-pull/dip volume; re-time all Upper
   sessions with transitions.
4. Give every loaded pancake exercise an RPE 5–6 ceiling and preserve the
   elevated fallback.
5. Add a technical fallback for the double-kettlebell clean/front squat and a
   competence gate for single-arm swings.
6. Make recovery spacing explicit for the self-paced run.
7. Either add a second low-dose impact exposure and recommended easy aerobic
   work, or narrow the cardiovascular/bone-health claims.
8. Publish realistic Lower/Upper durations or remove accessory volume.

After those changes, the nine-template structure would be a reasonable,
evidence-informed personal-first program to test and iterate. It should still
be described as **evidence-informed**, not “research-proven.”
